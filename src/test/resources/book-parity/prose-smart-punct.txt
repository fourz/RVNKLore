The road to Bankrekka was never kind to travellers. Old “Aunt” Marrow kept the lantern at the ford — she said it was for the ferryman, though nobody had seen a ferryman in forty years… Merchants called the crossing «the Long Water» and paid her in salt.

In the winter of the third age the river froze solid, and the caravans walked straight across. It’s said that Marrow wept, because the ice made her lantern useless; it’s also said she laughed. Both stories survive in the tavern songs – pick whichever suits the hour.

• Salt, iron and wool came north.
• Glass, books and rumour went south.

When the thaw came, the lantern was gone. Some say a pilgrim took it to the shrine at Ouroboros; others swear it still burns under the ice, waiting for a ferryman who will never row again.
